From Adoption to Governance

The decisive turn is no longer whether universities adopt AI, but how they govern it — even as the data shows adoption has already outrun guidance. Faculty and students use AI near-universally, yet fewer than a third of faculty feel equipped or involved. The next phase is assessment redesign, faculty enablement, and formal governance — not tool procurement.

Three priorities — fluency for every student, innovation across the enterprise, institutional leadership — backed by 67 faculty articles and 20 classroom lessons. A replicable model: survey what faculty already do, then codify it.

AI in Teaching & Learning

03 signals
01

Adoption is universal, guidance is not

88% of students and 77% of faculty use AI, but 57% of students say assessments carry inadequate AI guidance, only 31% of faculty feel involved in shaping policy, and 22% now find it harder to work without AI. The frontier has shifted to assessment redesign and instructor readiness.

EdTech Innovation Hub ↗
02

AI anxiety is now an enrollment and retention risk

47% of students have seriously considered changing majors because of AI and 16% already have, yet only 19% feel their program is current for an AI-enabled future. Curriculum currency must become a leadership priority.

Lumina–Gallup ↗
03

Instructors and employers agree on only 1 of 26 AI skills

Employers weight workflow redesign, automation, and technical integration; instructors emphasize critical thinking and AI limits. There is no shared, assessable framework — building one aligned with employers is a concrete, near-term lever.

AI Workforce & Skills

03 signals
01

Faculty capability is the binding constraint

Only 13% of leaders rate their faculty's AI knowledge "very strong," and inefficient education-to-work transitions cost the U.S. roughly $1.1 trillion a year. Upskilling faculty is the single highest-leverage investment.

Pearson ↗
02

The economy has moved to skills; institutions still signal roles

Workers oversupply generalist traits while employers seek specialized, technical, execution-oriented capabilities. Shift curricula toward job-ready, project-based skills — and coach students to signal specific skills.

03

AI job-ad alignment stays below 50% — even at MIT, Stanford, CMU

Program materials under-weight transferable skills and workplace tools while over-weighting theory. It is partly a signaling problem: advertise the skills employers actually pay for.

AI Policy & Operations

03 signals
01

EU AI Act: high-risk education deferred to Dec 2027 — but not everything

The Digital Omnibus pushed stand-alone high-risk obligations back ~16 months, while prohibited practices, AI literacy, and chatbot disclosure (Article 50) apply now. Don't conflate deferral with full delay.

CASRAI ↗
02

Admissions, grading, and proctoring are the highest-compliance-risk deployments

Under Annex III these trigger full obligations — risk management, data governance, human oversight — with penalties up to €15M or 3% of turnover. Audit and document them first.

Regumatrix ↗
03

Florida moves to require AI policies at all 28 state colleges

The proposed rule requires board-approved policies on use, integrity, grading, AI literacy, privacy, and parental notification — a State Board vote is set for September 16. A likely template for other states.

What this means for your institution

04 moves
01

Shift spend from tools to faculty enablement. The evidence is consistent across Pearson, DEC, and BCG: faculty capability — not procurement — is the binding constraint, and training lifts adoption by 14–19 points.

02

Treat assessment redesign and academic integrity as one program. Rewrite assessments to reward work AI can't do alone, paired with clear disclosure expectations rather than blanket bans.

03

Codify a shared, assessable AI-skills framework — and align it with employers. Define the skills, teach them, and advertise them; the gap is partly a signaling failure.

04

Start compliance now where it is already enforceable. AI literacy, transparency, and banned practices are live under the EU AI Act; audit admissions, grading, and proctoring first.
