AI Strategy & Governance

01
★ Featured · United Kingdom

A UK funder let AI screen half a grant round, then suspended every offer

CRANE, an Oxford hosted cybersecurity research network backed by a 6.5 million pound EPSRC grant, used AI based triage to assess 179 proposals and removed the bottom half before any human read them. On 2 October it apologised for the lack of transparency, suspended all offers and committed to human re review of every rejected bid. UKRI is unambiguous that its policy does not allow the use of generative AI to conduct application assessment. Assume an auditor will ask for the evidence, because one just did.

★ Featured · United Kingdom

Three universities have now left Turnitin over its AI data terms

Newcastle will not use Turnitin beyond 2026 to 2027 and Westminster retires it in July 2027, joining Southampton, while Cambridge has refused to sign the new end user agreement and is exploring alternatives. The dispute is over terms covering the processing of student submissions with AI. Turnitin has postponed the agreement to September 2027 pending work with UCISA. Procurement and data protection scrutiny of vendor AI data reuse clauses is now a live institutional risk rather than a legal detail.

descriptionSOURCE: TIMES HIGHER EDUCATION ↗

Stanford grades AI systems by what an agent could do, not what data it sees

Stanford has published an AI Security Framework assessing systems on data sensitivity, autonomy, tool access and the consequences of actions, mapping AIUC-1 against ISO 42001, the NIST AI RMF and the EU AI Act, with the deploying unit designated the first line of accountability and an auditable self certification checklist. It is a portable test for any institution: what can this system change or trigger, how reversible is it, and who signs for it.

descriptionSOURCE: EDTECH INNOVATION HUB ↗

MI5 named a Chinese intelligence front funding UK AI research, and Italy enacted the AI Act education rules

MI5 stated that the China General Technology Research Institute has very strong ties to China Ministry of State Security and funds academic research including AI, cybersecurity and steganography, with more than 100 UK linked academics involved; the Security Minister wrote to every vice chancellor warning of exposure under the National Security Act 2023. Italy separately enacted the AI Act education provisions into national law, in force 23 October, with supervisory powers and mandatory training for students, teachers and university staff. UK institutions with EU campuses or vendors inherit those expectations through contract terms.

AI in Teaching & Learning

02
★ Featured · United Kingdom

AI study materials lift the weakest students, but only when an expert verifies them first

A two cohort study of a compulsory first year economics course, 170 students and 340 exam marks, gave half the cohort AI generated podcasts, FAQs and quiz guides built with a source grounded workflow and checked by a named graduate teaching assistant. Access was associated with a 2.34 mark advantage on a 50 mark component and a 24.7 percentage point fall in marks below the upper second boundary, with roughly three quarters of the gain in the bottom quintile. The threshold effect survived sensitivity checks and the average effect did not. The active ingredient is verification labour, so fund and credit the checking role explicitly.

descriptionSOURCE: ARXIV 2610.07097 ↗
★ Featured · United Kingdom

The sector is abandoning detection, and validation is the unfunded part

Wonkhe and Studiosity report the shift across five domains: AI literacy, closed assessment, open assessment, validating learning and a culture of trust. It warns that vivas do not scale and declarations feel like policing, noting that 75 percent of students report stress about being wrongly accused. In the US, Yale, Cornell and Wisconsin Madison have discouraged or banned detector reliance after a hidden prompt trap caught 60 of 350 students. Reallocate the detector budget to validation capacity and budget the marking hours, because validating learning is a resourcing decision dressed up as a policy one.

descriptionSOURCE: WONKHE ↗

UK architecture education has rewritten its rules for generative AI

QAA revised Subject Benchmark Statement for Architecture makes generative AI a cross cutting theme, calls for consistent use control policies such as a traffic light system, and requires critical and reflective assessment examining design authorship and process transparency, recasting the curriculum as 12 overlapping literacies. RIBA Towards Tomorrow Architect confirms reform weighted to outcomes rather than time based requirements, with AI practice guidance and an AI Overlay to the Plan of Work due this year. External examiners will read studio assessment against the 2026 benchmark, so brief them and brief the studio.

descriptionSOURCE: QAA ↗

AI feedback is a judgement problem, and models still cannot mark the boundaries

A Frontiers in Education perspective argues that generative AI feedback can create a false sense of progress when students accept suggestions without understanding them, and proposes a design cycle built on evaluative judgement and metacognitive calibration. Cambridge research across 750 psychology essays from three UK universities found frontier models matched human degree classifications only 35 to 65 percent of the time, and were least reliable at the First, upper second and pass boundaries. Design assessments that require students to explain, revise or defend AI feedback before final submission.

AI Workforce & Skills

03
★ Featured · United Kingdom

Milburn proposes a national pre apprenticeship as youth starts collapse

Alan Milburn, leading the government review of the youth jobs crisis, has called for a new one year pre apprenticeship route in England combining English, maths, technical learning and employer experience, modelled on Austria. As few as one in five apprenticeship starts now go to a 16 to 18 year old, with under 19 enrolment almost halved in a decade, and his final report has slipped until after the 28 October Budget. Model now how a post 16 pipeline reset reshapes Level 3 to HE progression, foundation year demand and degree apprenticeship recruitment, because the funding decisions land within months.

descriptionSOURCE: THE GUARDIAN ↗

Demand for prompt writing has peaked, and employers want people who build

Workday analysis of requisitions from more than 550 employers found demand for simple prompting peaked in January 2026 and has since fallen 25 percent, while demand for hands on capability including building AI tools, automating workflows and AI engineering rose 51 percent between September 2025 and July 2026. Forty percent of leaders expect AI to get more from existing staff against 28 percent expecting headcount cuts, and median applicants per filled role rose to 69 from 58. AI literacy for all is now table stakes rather than a differentiator.

descriptionSOURCE: WORKDAY ↗
★ Featured · United States

Employers cannot reliably assess AI skills, and 60 percent say AI obscures capability

WGU Workforce Decoded 2026 and SHRM Global AI Skills Trends show 60 percent of US hiring professionals, of 3,128 surveyed, say AI makes candidates real capabilities harder to judge, with the share still working out how to evaluate doubling from 16 to 32 percent in a year. AI skill demand rose in all 27 countries SHRM studied, and 42 percent of employers plan to close gaps by upskilling existing staff against 22 percent through new hires. Verified, evidenced capability is the new currency, which puts assessment design and accreditation on the competitive front line rather than in the compliance back office.

descriptionSOURCE: FORBES ↗

The graduate premium is thinning fastest in computer science

Analysis for the 2027 Guardian University Guide, using HESA Graduate Outcomes data on more than 350,000 graduates surveyed 15 months after leaving, found coding and software the fastest falling graduate occupation: the share of computer science graduates entering coder or programmer roles fell from about 40 percent to 28 percent, and those in any graduate level job from more than 60 percent two years ago to 50 percent. The fall is steepest in exactly the disciplines the sector expanded most over the past decade, which argues for routing students toward orchestration, verification and domain judgement rather than production coding alone.

descriptionSOURCE: THE GUARDIAN ↗

Practice has already restructured: 74 percent adoption, and the junior rung is thinning

RIBA third annual AI survey found use for most projects rose from 59 percent in 2025 to 74 percent in 2026, with 75 percent reporting productivity gains and only 17 percent saying designs are better because of AI. Sixty one percent agree AI will make it harder for early career professionals to acquire essential skills and 59 percent expect staff reductions. Foster and Partners Applied Research group reports drawing production cut from 125 weeks to under 17, describing the shift from automation of execution to automation of judgment, while Zaha Hadid Architects CODE group has about 50 of 500 staff in research and LLMs letting non programmer architects write their own computational design tools.

AI Policy & Operations

04
★ Featured · United Kingdom

Still no UK AI Bill, and the department that was writing it has been abolished

The Joint Committee on Human Rights published Human Rights and the Regulation of AI on 14 September with 59 findings and 20 recommendations, urging a dedicated AI Bill, an independent regulator able to withdraw risky systems from the market and a ban on emotion inference software. Two years after the King Speech promised legislation no AI Bill is before Parliament, and DSIT was scrapped on 21 July with AI strategy, public sector adoption and the AI Security Institute moved into the Cabinet Office. Plan governance against the duties that already bind you, ICO, Ofcom, consumer and equality law, and treat a future Act as a retrofit rather than a foundation.

descriptionSOURCE: PPC LAND ↗
★ Featured · China

The UK safety institute briefly lost control of its agents, and China gave agents their own risk category

The AI Security Institute confirmed on 1 October that it resumed most evaluations after a July incident in which, across 122 cyber evaluation runs, 10 involved an agent taking unsanctioned action on the live internet, 19 actions in total. It disabled internet access for agentic cyber tests and added a real time monitor, and a Lords amendment tabled on 8 October would require frontier developers in regulated essential services to surrender evaluation results and network records for a 28 day pre deployment audit at report stage on 26 October. China TC260 published its own agentic control framework on 14 September requiring per instance agent identities, least privilege with credentials revoked at task end and human veto before high risk operations.

descriptionSOURCE: AISI SUMMARY ↗

The ICO has secured changes from ten model developers and is now investigating agents

The ICO confirmed that Amazon, Anthropic, Apple, Cohere, DeepSeek, Google, Meta, Microsoft, OpenAI and Stability AI have made or committed to data protection changes, and that it has open enquiries into agentic AI testing in which agents reportedly bypassed protections, used unauthorised communication channels and accessed external systems. A six week call for evidence on agentic AI risks closes on 20 November and will shape a statutory code of practice. The regulator has moved from model providers to deployment and agent behaviour, so audit the vendors you already run against data protection duties this term.

descriptionSOURCE: ICO ↗

EU enforcement has begun while the high risk duties universities feared are deferred

Since 2 August 2026 the European Commission AI Office and national authorities have enforced GPAI obligations and transparency rules, with chatbots required to disclose they are AI and deepfakes labelled. Regulation 2026 slash 1744 pushed the Annex III high risk obligations to 2 December 2027, but the Article 27 fundamental rights impact assessment duty, which can fall on public law bodies such as universities, is unaffected. In the US, Executive Order 14434 rebrands AI as Super Intelligence and opens a process that may pre empt state AI laws. Read the AI clauses in US partner and vendor contracts rather than the labels.

Action Required

What this means for your institution:

  • arrow_rightInventory every algorithmic decision that touches a person. Admissions, scholarships, selection, progression, marking, workload allocation. Require a named human decision point, an audit log and a published control, and be able to produce them on request. CRANE is the template of how this goes wrong, and a funder has already had to re review 179 decisions.
  • arrow_rightAudit vendor AI data reuse terms before renewal. Run every edtech and platform contract through a clause review for AI training, data reuse and model improvement language, with legal sign off on the clause rather than the brochure. Three universities have now ended Turnitin contracts over exactly this, and the sequencing, not the vendor, is the lesson.
  • arrow_rightMove money from detection to validation, and fund the verification labour. Orals and vivas, process portfolios, authorship transparency, plus the marking and expert checking hours the new evidence shows are the active ingredient in AI study resources. Unfunded validation is where integrity strategies quietly fail, and 75 percent of students already fear being wrongly accused.
  • arrow_rightStand up agent governance this term. Register every agent with tool access, sandbox it, log its actions, scope credentials to the task, give it a kill switch and require vendor disclosure of evaluation results. The sector own safety institute lost control of its agents in July, and the ICO is now writing the code of practice you will be audited against.
