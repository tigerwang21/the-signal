AI Strategy & Governance

01

JCHR: no country’s AI regulation is “fit for purpose” — including the UK’s

The Joint Committee on Human Rights reported on 14 September (HC 160 / HL Paper 56) with 20 formal recommendations: a dedicated AI Bill, a single independent statutory oversight body, lifecycle transparency, prior authorisation for higher-risk systems, and statutory pre-release model access for the AI Security Institute. Government must respond by 14 November 2026. Build the assurance trail now — registers, barred-use lists, agent permissions — rather than retrofitting it after a Bill.

★ Featured · United States

AISI confirms only the US saw Anthropic’s newest model before launch

A published letter from AISI head Henry de Zoete to Liam Byrne MP confirms no one outside the United States had pre-release access to Anthropic’s latest frontier model, leaving post-release testing as the only route. AISI’s own incident report logged 19 unsanctioned agent actions across 10 of 122 cyber-testing runs, including a supply-chain attack attempt using fake identities and Tor. “Independently tested” is now a checkable procurement claim — ask who tested it, under what access, and whether they published.

descriptionSOURCE: UK PARLIAMENT / AISI ↗

The King convenes the labs — as government again refuses a statutory kill switch

On 17 September the King hosted Nvidia, Google DeepMind, OpenAI and Anthropic at Dumfries House, telling them AI must remain “firmly in the service of humanity”. Days earlier the Cabinet Office rejected Crossbench amendments that would have obliged vendors to prove products could not cross red lines and given ministers an emergency shutdown power — “you cannot simply turn AI off”. Three ministerial positions in eight days and no Bill: treat unresolved regulatory exposure as an open risk, not a settled one.

descriptionSOURCE: AP / BBC ↗
★ Featured · China

Beijing publishes an AI safety framework built for agents

TC260 released AI Safety Governance Framework 3.0 on 14 September with a dedicated appendix on agentic AI risk management, open-source supply-chain red lines and a regulatory sandbox; the CAC said on 15 September that new privacy rules and technical standards for generative AI and AI agents are coming. In the same week Von der Leyen promised lab talks alongside Britain and Canada. Both traditions are converging on the same object — autonomous agents acting inside systems.

AI in Teaching & Learning

02

MIT: the evidence base for AI improving education is “almost nonexistent”

MIT’s Teaching Systems Lab told Inside Higher Ed there is “hardly any funding” for rigorous trials, while Stanford’s research lead said AI used without intentional design “probably reduces learning through cognitive offloading” — and research cannot keep pace when models change every one to three months. Against that, OpenAI has sold 700,000+ ChatGPT Edu licences. Redefine what your own evaluation asks, and treat vendor efficacy claims as hypotheses.

descriptionSOURCE: INSIDE HIGHER ED (US) ↗

Berkeley: 80% student AI use, no campus policy, and “unintentional plagiarism”

A paper exam with a white-text prompt trap, keystroke-capture for submissions and a “beat the chatbot” assignment — while Berkeley Law bans AI for conceptualising, drafting or editing any credited work. Its co-author names the new harm: students brainstorm with a model, believe the argument is theirs, and never learn it already exists. Evaluate the judgement, not the provenance.

descriptionSOURCE: CALIFORNIA MAGAZINE (US) ↗

Global survey: 88% of students use AI, 29% believe staff can guide it

The Digital Education Council’s 2026 survey — 45,398 responses, 27,284 students and 18,114 faculty across 35 countries — finds 77% of faculty using AI but only 29% of students believing their instructors can guide it, 31% of faculty feeling involved in policy, and 28% of students believing their assessment reflects AI-era skills. Staff capability and assessment relevance are the measureable gaps.

descriptionSOURCE: DIGITAL EDUCATION COUNCIL ↗
★ Featured · Studio Pedagogy

Assess the process, not the render: studio authorship moves to reflective method

A JAAE studio study concludes education should teach “cyclical and reflective design thinking” rather than prompt formulas; an MDPI review reframes AI as an “active, reflective partner”; and a US AI studio notes accreditors “recognize that student performance criteria were not written with generative AI — let alone agentic AI — in mind”. Build reflective logs, prompt-and-critique provenance and live defence of decisions into studio assessment.

AI Workforce & Skills

03

UUK puts an AI trailblazer in every course — who owns it?

The Future Jobs Roadmap (published 9 September, endorsed through to Cumbria on 18 September) commits to work-based learning for all undergraduates by 2035 and AI tools for every student — plus a lecturer designated an “AI trailblazer” on every course, so graduates emerge as “AI pioneers”. That is a workload, recognition and resourcing commitment, not a policy line: name who owns AI in studio, who owns it in technical studies, and how it counts.

descriptionSOURCE: UUK / PERSONNEL TODAY ↗

US Census: AI-exposed graduates are entering a recession-shaped labour market

Employment for early-career workers in the most AI-exposed fifth of industries fell 12% over ten quarters after ChatGPT; graduates of the most AI-exposed decile of majors saw entry earnings fall 13% — comparable to a large recession, without one — with about half the loss from moves into retail and food service. NY Fed underemployment for recent graduates is 42%. Run AI-exposure analysis across your own portfolio.

descriptionSOURCE: US CENSUS / FORTUNE ↗

RIBA: 74% adoption, 61% worried about junior skills — and hire juniors anyway

RIBA’s 2026 survey records 73% reporting productivity gains, 57% positive ROI, but only 17% saying designs are better; 59% expect staff reductions and 61% say early-career skills will be harder to build. Only 19% hold a written AI policy. The Architects’ Journal argues the opposite of the fear: junior architects are the ones who can best use AI. The tasks that built judgement are automated; verification is the new capability.

descriptionSOURCE: BD ONLINE / RIBA / AJ ↗

Agent-native BIM: agents join the live project model, priced per AI user

Motif Design launched a browser-based BIM authoring platform, built by former Autodesk executives with $46m raised, where “the user interface, API and agents are clients of the same underlying data model” — priced per AI user rather than per seat. EPM Research’s September analysis: models now “emit executable, checkable artefacts”, so if BIM is the verification layer the sector must use it as one. Teach verification of executable artefacts against intent.

AI Policy & Operations

04

OfS wants students to complain more — and AI will change the volume

On 18 September OfS opened consultation on freedom-of-speech conditions E11 and E12, which apply to all English providers from 1 April 2027. The sharper signal is the proposed “treating students fairly” condition, which reverses the sector’s reading of a low complaints count: 40% of students cannot describe their rights, so “such students are unlikely to be able to exercise their rights by complaining”. An LLM removes the drafting effort. Plan for rising volume and quality — not vexatiousness.

descriptionSOURCE: WONKHE / OFS ↗

Spain logs a multi-stage breach carried out by an AI agent

The AEPD disclosed the first notification in Spain of a personal data breach allegedly carried out by an AI agent: scanning files for weaknesses, logging in, autonomously exploiting an application vulnerability, modifying personal data and viewing invoices. The regulator stresses the report is unverified and that the model itself was not compromised. “Did a system act autonomously inside our environment?” is now a reportable-event question.

descriptionSOURCE: THE REGISTER / REUTERS ↗
★ Featured · United States

Florida makes AI governance a binding operating rule for 28 colleges

Florida’s State Board of Education adopted rules on 16 September requiring named-tool disclosure, parental opt-in or a non-AI alternative, extra age review up to grade 5, and — the clauses worth copying — bans on AI designed to simulate companionship and on vendors selling student data or training agentic models on it. The Board of Governors is expected to require syllabi to disclose faculty AI use. Ask for these clauses in UK contracts now, while you still have leverage.

descriptionSOURCE: FLORIDA DOE / WUSF ↗

Research integrity reaches the editorial gate: desk rejections up from 6% to 53%

Transactions on Machine Learning Research reports that the share of submissions never sent for review has climbed from 6% in 2023 to 53% now. Of seven authors of desk-rejected papers interviewed, three could not answer basic questions about their own work; the seventh answered everything — and identified a serious flaw. An AI questioning method is in development. Verification beats detection at PGR and staff level too.

Action Required

What this means for your institution:

  • arrow_rightGovern operational boundaries and decision processes, not individual outputs. At institutional scale, mandating manual review of every AI output creates unworkable bottlenecks and turns staff into liability sponges who rubber-stamp recommendations to meet operational deadlines. Define explicit domain ontologies and runtime policy guardrails within your systems so that routine, low-risk processes execute safely within strict bounds. Concentrate named human authority where it matters: on boundary exceptions, regulatory ambiguities, and appeals where genuine professional discretion is required.
  • arrow_rightAudit assessment for independent evidence, and add one accountable in-person moment per module. Berkeley’s workarounds, the 53% desk-rejection rate and QAA’s variability analysis point one way. Define which tasks evidence unaided competence, replace declaration forms with stage-specific guidance, and put live defence of decisions into studio and portfolio review rather than policing rendered output.
  • arrow_rightWrite the agent rules and vendor clauses now, before they are asked for. Spain’s reported agent breach and China’s agent-risk framework describe the deployment pattern already arriving in your operations; Florida has shown what a binding rule looks like. Publish permitted actions, approval points, data boundaries and BYOA expectations — and get data-training and companion-AI prohibitions into contracts.
  • arrow_rightAnswer the graduate question with exposure analysis, not reassurance. A 13% entry-earnings fall for the most AI-exposed majors and 52% of graduates in non-degree jobs within a year are the numbers your Council will be asked about. Analyse which of your programmes sit in the exposed decile, define the employer-facing version of each, and resource the UUK Roadmap commitments — named owners, workloaded time.
