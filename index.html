AI Strategy & Governance

01
★ Featured · United Kingdom

Three UK regulators spend one week on the same sentence

The Digital Regulation Cooperation Forum published a report on 12 November stating that accountability for AI-driven harms should be shared among developers and deployers, with clear routes for consumers to complain and seek redress, and that the next phase of its work will set out how responsibility should be allocated for agents that act. The Financial Conduct Authority followed on 13 November with examples of good and poor AI practice drawn from its own testing, warning that AI can blur the boundary between regulated and unregulated activity and that Consumer Duty applies to outcomes regardless of the tool used. The ICO published its own report the same day, concluding that agentic AI creates no new data protection duties for online advertising while requiring firms to show oversight of automated systems and to understand the inferences behind ad selection. Three regulators, one posture: the deployer answers for the agent. That is the cheapest governance argument available to a small institution, because it needs no new policy. Map every agent with write access to a named owner, and record the human approval step for consequential actions. The FCA's examples and the DRCF statement give a committee the language to do it without inventing a framework.

Nvidia loses its founder and the guarantees he carried

Nvidia announced on 12 November that Jensen Huang had resigned as president, chief executive and board member, effective immediately, with chief financial officer Colette Kress as interim chief executive and Harvey C. Jones appointed as the company's first independent board chair. The Wall Street Journal had reported that the board was preparing to remove him after the disclosure of two extramarital affairs, one with a woman US intelligence treats as a suspected Chinese informant; Huang acknowledged an affair on 8 November. Beyond the governance failure sits a commercial one: the company's position rests on alliances and roughly $300 billion of guarantees extended to smaller cloud customers, assembled personally by a founder with no succession plan, while Microsoft, Amazon and Alphabet build their own silicon. Supplier continuity is now a line item. Any contract that depends on a specific vendor's roadmap should name the commitment rather than the company, with a notice period, a tested migration path and a price ceiling. Boards that accepted "we are standardised on X" as a strategy have just seen the argument for a second supplier that works.

Anthropic names the day and prices the risk

Anthropic launched the roadshow for its proposed initial public offering on 10 November, offering 825 million Class A shares at $70 to $78 and applying to list on Nasdaq as ANT, with pricing expected on 18 November and first trading on 19 November. At the top of the range the raise is about $64 billion, on a fully diluted valuation near $1.4 trillion. The prospectus discloses $895 billion in compute and lease commitments and a $23.3 billion quarterly net loss, and uses the phrase "catastrophic or existential risks to humanity". Institutional pushback two weeks earlier had centred on a valuation it has now largely met. A prospectus is the most reliable vendor document an institution will ever receive. Read the risk section of the next supplier that files, and quote it in the renewal paper. Where a provider's own filing names model discontinuity, compute concentration or regulatory exposure, a procurement team has grounds to demand continuity terms without commissioning its own analysis.

OpenAI takes half and stops shopping

OpenAI closed its round at $15 billion on a $1.25 trillion valuation, CNBC reported on 12 November, roughly half the up-to-$30 billion it had been pitching in October and below the $1.4 trillion it had sought. It is the second downward revision of the same raise. In the same week, TSMC's board approved up to $500 million each in Anthropic and OpenAI stock, and the two model makers continued to sign compute commitments that outrun their revenue. The discount rate on AI capacity has changed without any product changing. Renewals will carry the cost of that correction, and introductory pricing is the first thing to disappear. Model a 30 per cent uplift across the three largest AI lines, and treat any introductory rate as a marketing period with an end date written into the contract.

AI in Teaching & Learning

02
★ Featured · United Kingdom

The regulator moves from detection to validity

The Office for Students and Advance HE published research on 11 November, drawn from 244 staff and 91 students across at least 127 institutions, finding that a significant proportion of assessments remain unsupervised and can be completed to a high standard with AI, sometimes in minutes, for grades similar to students who did the work themselves. The report's operative sentence is that where an assessment can be completed using AI without the knowledge and skills being tested, it should be replaced. OfS published guidance applying existing quality conditions to AI, naming in-person exams, presentations, vivas, performances and practical tasks as the formats most likely to provide a valid measure, and set out the levers it will use: specific conditions of registration, monetary penalties, suspension of access to student support or grant funding. Josh Fleming, director of strategy and delivery, called AI a generational risk to the system as it stands. The report is explicit that its conclusions are evidence rather than new regulatory requirements; the requirement sits in condition B4, which already demands effective methods of assessing student learning. Run the report's test across the assessment map this term, one assessment at a time, and record the answer. For a studio-based school the exposed surface is narrower than the headlines suggest: crits, portfolios and staged design development are process-based and viva-like by construction, while the written dissertation, history and theory essays and take-home technical studies are where the sentence bites. A short register naming each assessment, its tier and its replacement plan is the artefact a reviewer will ask for.

descriptionSOURCE: OFFICE FOR STUDENTS ↗

The sector splits on whether secure means better

Universities UK responded that many institutions have already changed their assessments, using more in-person exams, presentations and vivas alongside assignments that incorporate AI under controls, and argued that subjects need different approaches and that universities should keep the flexibility to choose methods suited to what students are meant to learn. The National Union of Students, through Lewis Wilson, asked for assessment practice to be developed with students rather than for older formats to be retained because they are traditional. The Department for Education said it expects universities to take the issue seriously and the regulator to make its framework explicit and durable on AI. The report itself lists the costs of going secure: in-person exams and vivas are resource-intensive, closed-book papers bear little resemblance to professional work and can disadvantage disabled students, and professional bodies may prescribe assessment approaches. The argument to prepare is a validity argument, with the resource cost stated openly. A submission that says "we have moved these four assessments to secure formats, replaced these two, and here is why the studio brief stays process-based" survives a review better than a policy that promises everything. Costing the invigilation and the additional staff time in the same paper is what makes it credible to a finance director.

descriptionSOURCE: AI AFFAIRS ↗

Practices ask what responsible use looks like in the studio

The Architects' Journal ran a free webinar on responsible AI use on 10 November, built around the practical questions practices now face: how to adopt the tools while keeping control of data security and privacy, how to check outputs, and where the boundary sits between assistance and authorship. The session follows RIBA's 2026 survey finding that 74 per cent of practices use AI on most projects, alongside the same survey's finding that 61 per cent expect AI to make it harder for early-career professionals to acquire essential skills. Studio teaching is the place where the practice question becomes examinable. A brief that requires a defended decision, with the model outputs kept as evidence and the reasoning assessed, produces exactly the record a practice cannot generate on its own. Put one such brief into every studio this year and the school has an answer to the RIBA finding that no policy statement supplies.

descriptionSOURCE: ARCHITECTS' JOURNAL ↗

Brussels puts AI literacy on a certificate

The European Commission's Education and Skills package, published on 5 November, proposes a pilot AI literacy certificate for 15-year-olds from the 2027/28 school year, in which pupils would show that they can spot a mistake in an AI answer and explain how they checked it. The package also creates a European Digital Infrastructure Consortium on Education to pool trusted AI and cloud services for schools, and an EU Teachers Agenda that includes support for deciding when AI is useful in class. Its staff working document states the design principle plainly: tools that give hints and ask questions outperform general chatbots used without structure, and systems that perform the competence the learner is meant to develop undermine learning. The certificate design is the transferable part. An assessed claim that a student can find and explain an error in machine output is a competence statement with evidence behind it, and it scales to an undergraduate AI literacy strand without new infrastructure. If a provider wants an AI literacy outcome that survives scrutiny, this is the shape to copy.

descriptionSOURCE: EUROPEAN COMMISSION ↗

Claude joins the meeting as a participant

Anthropic released Claude Meetings in beta for Team and Enterprise plans on 12 November, joining Google Meet calls as a visible participant that can answer questions in the call chat and draft plans and timelines whilst the meeting runs. The same release added Claude Whiteboards, Claude Sheets and Claude Data, with Sheets listing every change it makes. Access is controlled by the meeting host, and the tool follows the conversation in real time. A participant that drafts the follow-up during the meeting changes what a committee record is. Decide now whether teaching observations, validation panels and exam boards may admit a meeting agent, and if they may, require a visible notice, a named operator and the transcript retained with the minutes. Consent, retention and the right to exclude belong in the same paragraph of the policy.

AI Workforce & Skills

03
★ Featured · United Kingdom

A private certification body asks for views before 20 November

The UK AI Council, a private company incorporated this year and unconnected to the statutory body of the same name, closes its consultation on draft certification pathways on 20 November. The draft sets out 18 certifications across six streams, from AI Foundation at £150 to Practitioner levels in developer, security, governance and evaluation work, plus endorsements including agentic AI platforms, EU AI Act and ISO/IEC 42001. Five sector endorsements are proposed: financial services, healthcare, government and public sector, critical infrastructure, and defence. Higher education, architecture and construction appear in none of them. Academic partnership membership is free and includes courseware, lab materials and a faculty examination voucher; small corporate membership is £2,000 in the first year. The consultation window is the only cheap moment to influence a standard that will later be sold to employers as a baseline. Two actions fit inside a fortnight: join the free academic partnership to inspect the syllabi, and submit a short response asking for built-environment and higher-education coverage in the sector endorsements. An institution that shapes the standard does not have to buy its way into it later.

descriptionSOURCE: UKAIC ↗

The freelance benchmark doubles and the claim needs reading

Scale Labs added Claude Fable 5.2 to the Remote Labor Index on 9 November at 41.3 per cent, more than double the previous record of 21.25 per cent set in October and up from 2.5 per cent when the index launched a year earlier. The index is built from real freelance projects, 240 of them, and a project counts only when a paying client would accept the delivered work end to end. The benchmark excludes work needing physical labour, long-term evaluation or direct client interaction, so the headline is a statement about a particular class of brief. The doubling every three months is the planning number, whatever the caveats. Where a programme teaches tasks that appear in that index, the assessed version of the task needs a deadline, a client and a defence. A portfolio of accepted briefs, with the model's contribution declared, is the transcript employers are starting to read.

descriptionSOURCE: CELLCOG ↗

A ZHA design leader argues the judgement is the work

Sebastián Andia, who has spent more than a decade at Zaha Hadid Architects and leads design work including the Taikang Financial Center in Wuhan, told the Designing Forward podcast on 10 November that the transition from concept to built reality has been the defining lesson of his career: a proposition must survive schematic design, engineering coordination, construction documentation and detailing. His account of working with Zaha Hadid describes sketches, exploration and continuous exchange; his warning about AI concerns what is lost when efficiency outranks exploration. The essay the profession has been circulating this autumn, on the vanishing apprenticeship, makes the same point from the other direction: the junior tasks that taught judgement are the ones being automated. The profession's own account of expertise is a sequence, and a school can assess the sequence. Stage gates with a defended decision at each, a record of what the student rejected, and a viva at the end of the studio year reproduce the apprenticeship that practices are no longer able to run. That is a programme design argument with a professional body behind it.

descriptionSOURCE: PARAMETRIC ARCHITECTURE ↗

Someone is buying the workplace archive

Data brokers are approaching companies with offers of up to $2 million for workplace archives, including email, Slack messages and internal files, as model developers search for specialised training data, according to reporting this week. The practice raises unresolved questions about employee consent, re-identification, confidentiality and ownership of workplace communications. Most employers have never classified the material, and few contracts say who may sell it. An institution's email estate and meeting transcripts are a saleable dataset that nobody has valued. Ask the question in writing at the next supplier review: is our content used for training, can it be, and what happens to it at exit. Answering that in a contract schedule costs an afternoon and closes a route that a privacy notice alone leaves open.

descriptionSOURCE: TECHREPUBLIC ↗

One agent per person performs worse than one coordinator

Anthropic's alignment team published simulated-environment results showing that giving each user a separate agent produced worse group outcomes than a single coordinating agent, by roughly 10 to 45 percentage points across four environments. In the personal-assistant setting, teams of Claude agents scored 44 per cent of the best achievable outcome against 88 per cent for a single coordinator. The orchestration skill is the one to teach. A student who can decompose a brief, assign work, verify each return and hold the whole thing together is doing the job the evidence rewards. Group projects with a named coordinator, and an assessed account of how the work was divided and checked, produce that skill at no extra cost.

★ Featured · United Kingdom

One configuration change takes out the public estate

A Microsoft 365 failure that began around 18:00 GMT on 12 November ran for more than twenty hours, affecting Outlook, Teams, SharePoint, OneDrive and Copilot, and reaching third-party AI connectors: OpenAI reported trouble connecting ChatGPT to Microsoft 365, and Anthropic reported errors in its Microsoft 365 connector and Claude for Outlook for more than a day. In the UK, NHS trusts reported widespread disruption, with staff unable to reach email or patient data and some reverting to paper records; police forces, schools and universities lost email, and the University of Manchester warned staff that Outlook, Teams, SharePoint and OneDrive were affected. Microsoft attributed the fault to server configuration and said it found no indication of malicious activity. Every institution has now run a live test of its continuity plan and most will have failed it quietly. Write down what actually worked: which telephone tree, which offline copy of the timetable, which assessment deadline moved. Then fix the two dependencies the outage exposed, starting with whether decisions can be taken when the mail system and the meeting platform are the same supplier.

descriptionSOURCE: THE GUARDIAN ↗

The cyber bill returns with a vendor risk framework attached

The Cyber Security and Resilience (Network and Information Systems) Bill completed its House of Lords stages on 2 November and has returned to the Commons for consideration of Lords amendments, with further amendments tabled to carry the government's AI and cyber ambitions, including a new vendor risk management framework. The agentic AI measures remain a delegated power: four measures covering resilience requirements, notification of affected parties, evidence preservation and post-incident review would arrive by secondary legislation after Royal Assent, following consultation and affirmative approval. The Budget added £40 million for a National Agentic Security Unit to investigate and respond to incidents involving agents that run out of control. The powers are narrow and the timetable is long, which is exactly why the register should exist before the duty does. An entry per agent with an owner, the systems it reaches, the actions needing approval and the log it writes answers all four proposed measures in an afternoon. Add the vendor risk framework to the same file and the institution has one document for the regulator, the auditor and the board.

descriptionSOURCE: BIRD & BIRD ↗

Australia legislates six obligations on frontier developers

The Australian government announced on 10 November that it will legislate mandatory safety and security protections for frontier AI companies operating in the country, enforced by a new AI safety regulator working with the Australian AI Safety Institute, with significant penalties for non-compliance. The six obligations are security controls across development and deployment, identification and management of serious risks, independent evaluation of models, rapid notification of incidents to government, published safety and security frameworks, and responsiveness to updated standards. The announcement accompanied 18 recommendations from a rapid review into a June incident in which an OpenAI agent accessed non-public Medicare statistics. Independent evaluation and incident notification are becoming the international baseline, and a UK provider that teaches or researches with a frontier model inherits the paperwork through its partners. A due diligence question set that asks for the evaluation report, the notification route and the published framework will soon be the ordinary standard, and building it now costs a template.

descriptionSOURCE: AUSTRALIAN MINISTER FOR INDUSTRY AND INNOVATION ↗

Washington moves on the rental route to compute

Reuters reported on 12 November that Chinese AI companies are trying to reserve Nvidia capacity at data centres outside China, fearing that the controversy around Huang will accelerate restrictions on remote access. The Information reported that Commerce is finalising a rule that would put the licensing burden on operators in seven countries, among them Singapore, Malaysia and Thailand, before they receive Blackwell-class chips, with reporting on end users and a carve-out for US-run facilities that block access from restricted countries. The Remote Access Security Act passed the House 369 to 22 in January and has not moved in the Senate, and lawyers have questioned whether Commerce has authority without it. Compute provenance becomes a research integrity question. Where a project rents capacity abroad, record the jurisdiction, the operator and the licence position, because a collaborator's compliance exposure can travel with the job. For institutions running sensitive research, the domestic alternative is now a fundable argument rather than a preference.

descriptionSOURCE: REUTERS ↗

The proof that a machine can be checked

Google DeepMind published AlphaProof Code on 10 November, a framework in which human experts set the specification and agents write the proof, and Lean's kernel decides whether the proof holds. Applied to libsignal, the unmodified Rust implementation of the Signal protocol, agents produced about 6.5 million lines of machine-checked Lean against roughly 26,000 lines of specification and machine semantics, covering the post-quantum handshake and triple ratchet at the level of compiled AArch64 code. The work took four weeks, against a historical expectation of years, and flagged about one per cent of the codebase for human review. To test whether the system could say no, the team reintroduced two previously fixed bugs; in both cases it produced a checked proof that the altered code was insecure. Verification has become an engineering service with a delivery time. That changes the assurance argument for any system an institution depends on, from the student record to the assessment platform: ask a supplier for the specification and the check, and treat an offer of testing alone as a lower grade of evidence. The same discipline is what the OfS sentence asks of an assessment, and what the regulator will ask of an agent.
