AI Strategy & Governance

01

The architect of the UK's AI strategy resigns as ARIA chair over his Anthropic role

Matt Clifford stepped down on 7 September after the Commons Science Committee chair called his plan to stay as founding ARIA chair while managing international affairs at Anthropic a "clear conflict of interest." He remains until 6 November under safeguards. Expect the same scrutiny of your own declarations — governors, advisory boards and national AI bodies now need published conflict-of-interest positions, not private assurances.

descriptionSOURCE: THE ↗

Two-in-five UK universities still have no AI policy anyone can find

HEPI Policy Note 71 scraped all 163 degree-awarding institutions: only 96 had discoverable policies, and 86% used the vocabulary of learning while operating as compliance instruments. A policy's location shapes its effect — one hosted in a misconduct framework inherits its assumptions about students. Audit policy discoverability and location before writing another word.

descriptionSOURCE: HEPI ↗

US: a universal AI course requirement announced before faculty governance approved it

At the University of Missouri, the president described an AI requirement for all undergraduates while the proposal had not cleared curriculum approval; USC cannot measure institution-wide AI emissions because vendors publish no emissions or water data. Sequence matters as much as content — take AI requirements through Senate and curriculum committees first.

Fudan runs 100+ AI courses and 41 dual degrees; the 2030 goal is literacy "vertically integrated across all stages." The strongest comparator for whether AI literacy becomes a formal graduate attribute.

AI in Teaching & Learning

02

"Reviewing AI-generated work is not a basic checking exercise"

Wonkhe's case from the University of Surrey makes the term's sharpest argument: supervise AI before learning the standards of the field and you get "fluency without competence." Surrey is embedding discipline-specific AI teaching into every degree programme from September 2026, refusing to treat AI as a generic skill or a bolt-on module. Decide, discipline by discipline, where AI builds judgement and where it substitutes for it.

descriptionSOURCE: WONKHE ↗

AI-assisted homework scores rise while unaided exam performance falls

Controlled studies put homework scores ~18% higher and time per assignment ~30% lower, with weaker later exam performance — the output/learning gap is now measurable. Tufts faculty conclude their governance was solving the wrong problem: assignments that no longer prove what a student knows. Audit which tasks still produce independent evidence, and rebuild those that no longer do.

descriptionSOURCE: AL JAZEERA ↗

Detection has failed as integrity infrastructure — Southampton leaves Turnitin

A controlled study (arXiv 2608.11256) finds guideline-compliant "refine only" edits flagged at 38–80%, while AI humanizers evade detection almost entirely — honest editing now carries higher sanction risk than deliberate evasion. Southampton will not renew its Turnitin contract over clauses allowing student submissions to train AI. Move integrity spend into assessment design and put data-training clauses in every contract.

descriptionSOURCE: ARXIV ↗

Studio culture is where the authorship question is actually being settled

Dezeen's survey of 53 leading studios finds 25 actively encourage AI use but only 19 have a written AI policy; more than half have built in-house tools and Claude is the most-used model. Denmark's Utzon Center opened an exhibition on human-vs-machine design, while Buffalo's Next-Gen Architect studio concluded authorship, not efficiency, is the unresolved question. Write the authorship standard into studio assessment.

descriptionSOURCE: DEZEEN ↗

AI Workforce & Skills

03

RIBA AI Report 2026: adoption tips over, and the profession worries about early careers

The third annual survey records adoption jumping from 59% to 74% of practices, alongside 59% expecting staff reductions and 61% saying AI will make it harder for early-career professionals to gain essential skills — only 17% say designs are better. RIBA will publish an AI Overlay to the Plan of Work and a responsible-use policy. For a school of architecture, design the replacement for the learning that used to happen in the drawing office.

descriptionSOURCE: RIBA ↗

Productivity gains are concentrated in image production, documentation and review

Zaha Hadid Architects reports renderings in 20% of the time and up to 50% productivity gains in mid-stage design; NVIDIA records 3x more iterative cycles. Foster + Partners warns of "review debt" — generative models producing hundreds of variations that mask embedded errors. Verification and judgement are the new graduate capability: teach checking and traceability, not prompt familiarity.

descriptionSOURCE: BUILDING DESIGN ↗

Agentic AI now targets studio operations, not just image-making

Avoice has launched what it presents as the first AI agents to run a studio's operational side — specification writing, drawing review, schedules, finances and invoicing — explicitly not design authorship. SOM reports "AI and agent-based workflows" for documentation automation. Professional education should weight systems integration, QA and regulatory reasoning.

descriptionSOURCE: DEZEEN ↗

Graduate demand narrows even as AI-specific entry routes open

UK employers are opening dedicated data-and-AI graduate programmes (Lloyds at £48,500; EY; Santander), while PwC puts the AI-skills wage premium at 56%. DSIT's AI Skills Boost has passed 1,001,147 completed courses, but only ~26% of architecture degree holders work as architects. Partner with the national upskilling programme and treat portfolio evidence of AI-augmented delivery as the employability asset.

AI Policy & Operations

04

The EU AI Act's education timetable is two dates — the nearer one is transparency

Article 4's AI-literacy obligation now requires providers and deployers to support AI literacy among staff, while Annex III high-risk uses (admissions, assessment, proctoring) are deferred to 2 December 2027. Article 50 transparency applies from 2 August 2026, and pre-market synthetic-content systems must comply by 2 December 2026. Document AI-literacy provision and label AI content and chatbots now.

descriptionSOURCE: SUBSTACK ↗

The ICO opens consultation on agentic AI guidance this month

The regulator's plan lists agentic AI guidance at drafting stage with consultation due September 2026 and a final version Spring 2027, alongside an automated-decision-making update this winter. Agent permissions, identity and liability are the next compliance surface in student services and research support — document agent scope now.

descriptionSOURCE: ICO ↗

ARB consults on a modernised Prescribed Examination — closing 16 September 2026

The proposals replace the Part 1/Part 2-era exam with an Application → Review → online Interview route assessed against ARB's new Academic Competency Outcomes, open to holders of a Level 7 qualification in architecture or a relevant discipline. This is the registration architecture assessment, portfolios and studio outcomes must map to — the final week to influence it.

descriptionSOURCE: ARB ↗

Agentic AI raises identity, equity and assurance questions — and silence creates risk

Jisc's National Centre for AI frames the decision as where human approval must always be required, proposing tightly scoped service accounts and agent-specific permissions. The student-facing version is BYOA — bringing your own agent — with equity risks where access to capable agents is uneven. Publish an agent policy this term and ask vendors for energy and water data in every AI procurement.
