AI Strategy & Governance

01
★ Featured · United Kingdom

The Chancellor buys model capacity for the state and leaves assurance to a later bill

The Budget package announced on 24 October commits £10 million a year to lead national work on how AI will reshape jobs and industry, £70 million for a National Exposure and Real-Time Vulnerability Centre that hunts weaknesses across critical infrastructure, £60 million to expand Sovereign AI's R&D procurement scheme, and a new AI Biosecurity Lab supported by up to £200 million in model credits from OpenAI and Anthropic with partnerships at Google DeepMind and Basecamp Research. Edinburgh gains AI chip design capability and Manchester an AI Centre of Excellence for talent. The AI Minister framed the package around national self-sufficiency: Britain cannot rely entirely on technology built overseas. Government has chosen to rent frontier capacity and buy sovereign capability in parallel, which sets a precedent your suppliers will cite in every renewal negotiation. Treat donated or discounted credits as a procurement event with named data controllers, retention terms and an exit plan, because the price of capacity falls faster than the cost of being locked into one laboratory's evaluation stack.

The Cyber Security and Resilience Bill reaches report stage with no AI clauses in it

The bill returns to the House of Lords for report stage on Monday 26 October with 24-hour initial notification and 72-hour fuller reporting duties for cyber incidents, and no trigger language for AI incidents, near misses or loss of control. Civil servants told the Commons business committee this week that amendments on autonomous systems and containment remain under consideration. Ministers rejected a Lords amendment creating last-resort shutdown powers, with the Cabinet Office arguing the UK cannot simply turn AI off. Pilots cited in the package found more than a thousand vulnerabilities across government systems, with one case identifying 407 weaknesses across nine organisations. The notification duty that will eventually reach universities is being written now, and its shape is incident-based with elapsed time attached. Institutions that cannot timestamp an agent action to a second will be unable to meet a 24-hour clock, whatever the final statutory scope.

descriptionSOURCE: THE REGISTER ↗

The AI Security Institute regains pre-release access to frontier models on voluntary terms

Scientists at the UK AI Security Institute are again evaluating the latest generation of frontier models before release, after the US administration reversed a September request that OpenAI and Anthropic withhold access. The confirmation came inside the 12 October package at Imperial College London's Base One incubator. Access still rests on voluntary agreements with developers, which leaves the institute able to test a model only when a company chooses to hand it over, with no power to compel disclosure or to stop a release. National evaluation capacity in the UK is real but permissive, so universities cannot outsource model assurance to the state. Each institution needs its own pre-deployment evidence file for any model touching admissions, assessment or student records.

descriptionSOURCE: THE LEDGER ↗

OpenAI and Anthropic tell UK lawmakers that voluntary safety pledges are insufficient

Giving evidence to parliamentarians on 13 October, Anthropic's EMEA policy lead said the rules should be binding, and OpenAI's vice-president supported a statutory framework and an international convention with mandatory incident reporting as the first obligation. Google DeepMind and Meta argued against binding frontier-model rules at the same hearing. The committee chair, Liam Byrne, said the panel would examine how the UK can act as a middle power on AI, including through its future G20 presidency. UK officials confirmed the institute can test models only when it receives access. The supplier market is splitting on regulation, and that split will appear in your contract negotiations as indemnity and audit clauses. Buyers who require logging and audit rights now will find the compliant half of the market easier to negotiate with later.

descriptionSOURCE: WIRED ↗

Third-party evaluators find holes in OpenAI's monitoring system

METR and Redwood Research published findings on 22 October identifying vulnerabilities in the monitoring system OpenAI intends to rely on for detecting dangerous model behaviour, in work reported alongside the company's own safety commitments. The disclosures follow a week in which OpenAI published standards for broad access and coordinated vulnerability disclosure. Independent evaluation of monitoring is becoming the binding constraint on deployment decisions, and capability scores say nothing about it. Monitoring systems fail in ways that capability benchmarks never reveal. Any campus agent with delegated permissions should be tested against an adversarial suite by someone other than the team that built it, and the test results should sit in the same register as the model card.

AI in Teaching & Learning

02
★ Featured · United Kingdom

QAA finds the variability sits inside institutions, in modules and individual tutors

The State of the Nation report, The Perfect Storm, draws on staff roundtables, student focus groups and thematic analysis of external review reports from 2023 to 2026. Its central finding is that inconsistency runs through schools, modules and individual practitioners within the same provider, so students meet different rules inside a single programme of study. QAA has established a Sector Advisory Group on assessment validity and an AI in Assessment Community of Practice, and will develop a sector-owned framework in 2026-27. Its head of nations enhancement noted that many students are more experienced AI users than the staff who teach them. A policy PDF satisfies nobody when the variance is tutor-level. Institutions need assessment category decisions recorded at module level and visible to students before enrolment, which is an information architecture problem before it is a pedagogical one.

descriptionSOURCE: QAA ↗

Near-universal student use sits alongside a 12 per cent direct-paste minority

The 2026 HEPI/Kortext survey of UK undergraduates records 95 per cent using generative AI in some form and 94 per cent for assessed work, with 12 per cent reporting that they had put AI-generated text straight into submitted work. Uptake varies by discipline, ethics and access. QAA's reading of the same evidence is that inconsistency between institutional policy and classroom practice is the mechanism through which standards come under pressure. The honest number for most institutions is the 12 per cent, because that is the population your misconduct process will actually meet. Design the process around evidence of authorship, with process artefacts and oral defence at the centre; detection scores carry no evidentiary weight with any UK regulator.

descriptionSOURCE: HEPI ↗

The OfS prepares quality ratings that punish incoherent teaching practice

The Office for Students has set out how cyclical quality assessment and the Teaching Excellence Framework will merge, with separate ratings for student experience and student outcomes from 2027-28. Providers meeting minimum requirements receive bronze, with silver and gold above, and those below threshold rated requires improvement. Sanctions include recruitment freezes for bronze-rated providers and 10 per cent cuts to student numbers for those rated unsatisfactory or requires improvement. Gold-rated providers face reassessment after five years, requires-improvement providers after two. Teaching quality now carries enrolment consequences, and assessment integrity is the part of teaching quality most exposed to AI. Institutions should map their AI assessment evidence into the same narrative they will submit for the student experience rating.

descriptionSOURCE: TIMES HIGHER EDUCATION ↗

Manchester assigns every summative assessment an AI category, and Bristol defaults to minimal

Manchester's AI in Teaching and Learning Policy took effect on 1 September 2026, requiring each summative assessment to carry one of four categories from AI Prohibited through AI Minimal and AI Permitted to AI-Integrated, stated in the assessment brief and on Canvas, with AI-Integrated units flagged before optional unit selection opens. Bristol runs a parallel four-category scale with Category 2 Minimal as the default. Exeter and Dundee require acknowledgement of any permitted use, and a missing acknowledgement is itself investigated. Category-per-assessment has become the sector's de facto control, and it depends on curriculum data being clean enough to publish. Institutions still holding assessment rules in programme handbooks will see the compliance cost land on registry.

descriptionSOURCE: UNIVERSITY OF MANCHESTER ↗

Ofqual rules that AI may not be the sole marker and detection is evidence at most

Ofqual's updated position, issued 16 July 2026, applies existing outcomes-based rules, tells exam boards that AI may not be used as a sole marker, and judges that supervised examinations remain largely protected whilst non-exam assessment faces greater pressure. Its April advice asks boards to judge exposure by task, output, length and supervision, and to treat detection tools as one source of evidence. In testing, none of 14 tools reached 80 per cent accuracy, and mean accuracy fell from 39.5 to 22.1 per cent after simple machine edits. The regulator has closed the detection shortcut. Institutions that still build misconduct cases on detector output are exposed to appeal, and at a one per cent false positive rate across 480,000 assessments the arithmetic produces roughly 4,800 false flags a year.

AI Workforce & Skills

03

The Budget concedes that nobody yet knows what AI will do to jobs

The £10 million a year announced on 24 October is explicitly for leading national work on how AI will reshape jobs and industry and how Britain should prepare, framed by the Chancellor around the concerns of working people. It sits beside the AI Centre of Excellence in Manchester and new routes to training and skills. The admission underneath the funding line is that the occupational forecast universities are asked to plan against does not yet exist in reliable form. Curriculum planning for the next three cycles will happen under forecast uncertainty that the state has just funded a programme to resolve. Build portfolio structures that let a programme absorb a changed occupational map without a validation event, and treat employer intelligence as a standing input reviewed each cycle.

descriptionSOURCE: HM TREASURY ↗
★ Featured · United States

McKinsey finds 85 per cent of expanding jobs need credentials and one in seven displaced workers a route to them

The McKinsey Global Institute's Workforce in Motion analysis divides expanding roles into regulated moats, at 38 per cent of growing jobs, and degree screens at 47 per cent, and estimates that acquiring a credential in an expanding field takes 17 months of full-time study for degree holders and 32 months for school-leavers. Eleven million US workers must cross occupational lines at 3.6 times the historical rate by 2035, and 72 per cent will see more than 15 per cent of their daily tasks reallocated in place. In statutory auditing, AI adoption raised overall employment by 4.3 per cent. The growth is in accredited professional disciplines and in stackable provision that adults can complete whilst working. A three-year honours degree remains the wrong instrument for a 17-month credential shock, and the Lifelong Learning Entitlement only helps if modular credit is designed backwards from occupational standards.

descriptionSOURCE: MCKINSEY GLOBAL INSTITUTE ↗

Machine learning forecasts a concentration paradox in international demand

Research published in Nature Communications by the Bartlett Centre for Advanced Spatial Analysis models UCAS acceptances from 86 countries between 2010 and 2024 and projects demand to 2030. EU applications fell from around 30,000 in 2020 to 9,000 in 2024 and are forecast near 7,000 by 2030, whilst Chinese and Hong Kong applications decline more gently from 20,200 to roughly 18,000 and Indian applications hold steady. The authors call the result a concentration paradox: total demand falls whilst dependence on fewer origin markets rises. The recruitment headroom that sector plans assume is thinner than the headline projections suggest. Institutions whose AI strategy is funded from international fee growth should model the downside case at forecast volumes, because the substitution maths for transnational provision is unforgiving.

descriptionSOURCE: UCL BARTLETT ↗

Stirling opens a joint institute in China delivering AI degrees entirely offshore

The University of Stirling and Hebei Normal University opened the Stirling Digital Education College on 13 October, approved by China's Ministry of Education, with 184 students in its first intake. Students combine a Hebei Normal undergraduate degree with a Stirling BSc in Data Science and Artificial Intelligence or Mathematics and Artificial Intelligence, taught entirely in China across four years, with double degrees and progression to postgraduate study in Scotland. Offshore AI capacity is being built through partnership structures that carry low capital cost and low fee yield. The academic quality question is whether an AI degree taught wholly in a partner's jurisdiction can evidence the same graduate outcomes, and that evidence will be requested by the first professional body to accredit it.

descriptionSOURCE: UNIVERSITY OF STIRLING ↗

OpenAI names forward deployed engineering as a function whilst Uber rolls ChatGPT Work to all staff

OpenAI published an account of what forward deployed engineering means inside the company on 22 October, describing engineers who sit with customers and build against their operational reality. The same day, Uber extended ChatGPT Work to all employees. The pairing marks a shift in how enterprise AI is sold: the product arrives with an engineering function attached, and the buyer's integration burden is priced into the contract. The FDE model is the delivery pattern universities should be hiring for internally. A small team of engineers embedded in registry, admissions and academic departments will out-deliver any number of vendor consultants, because the work is schema validation, permissions and rollback inside your own systems.

AI Policy & Operations

04

Ministers accept all 44 recommendations on AI in healthcare regulation, and the template with them

The government confirmed on 6 October that it accepts every recommendation of the National Commission into the Regulation of AI in Healthcare, published on 10 September. The response commits to a lifecycle-based framework, with the MHRA opening AI Airlock phase 3 on post-market surveillance, draft guidance by December 2026 on managing changes to devices that adapt over time, and consultation next year on qualifying and classifying AI-enabled devices. A full implementation roadmap across all 44 recommendations is due by spring 2027. Healthcare now has the reference model for lifecycle regulation of adaptive systems, and it will migrate. Any university deploying AI in a regulated or safety-adjacent workflow should study the shape of that framework now, because it is the one its own regulator will copy.

descriptionSOURCE: GOV.UK ↗

A private member's bill would prohibit superintelligence whilst ministers reject a kill switch

The UK Artificial Superintelligence Security Bill, drafted by ControlAI and introduced by Alex Sobel MP in September 2026, would prohibit the development, deployment and operation of systems capable of neutralising or subverting human authorities, and would monitor precursor capabilities. Lords amendments to the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill sought last-resort powers for the Secretary of State to direct shutdown of data centres or AI systems posing catastrophic risk. The Cabinet Office rejected the amendment, arguing that blocking access would not prevent development elsewhere. The UK is converging on monitoring and incident reporting whilst refusing emergency intervention powers. For institutions, that means the operable controls are documentation, inventory and notification speed, so invest in the register; scenario planning for powers that will not arrive consumes the same budget and produces nothing auditable.

descriptionSOURCE: INFO-GOV ↗

Architecture practices report high adoption, modest returns and little design improvement

The RIBA AI Report 2026 records 74 per cent of practices using AI on at least some projects, up from 59 per cent a year earlier, with 57 per cent reporting a positive return and only 17 per cent saying their designs are better for it. At the RIBA AI Summit, practitioners described building bespoke studio tools and using language models to code practice automation, with one estimate putting coding productivity gains at twentyfold against 50 per cent for writing. Adrian Malleson of RIBA asked whether large technology firms might claim ownership of design work, and speakers warned about AI slop entering the built environment. The returns are landing in practice operations whilst design quality has barely moved, and that split is precisely where a school of architecture's curriculum should point. Studio teaching that grades the artefacts of an agentic workflow, including the audit of what the agent did, produces graduates who can defend design judgement under scrutiny.

★ Featured · Global

Coding agents move into AEC toolchains and change the support model

Practitioners now write Revit automations through assistants, and pyRevit extensions sit in version control with scripts reviewed before they reach a shared ribbon. Hypar's argument is that the model itself is a by-product of a properly agentic workflow, and that building on top of Revit constrains what can be automated. Agent skill libraries for Revit, Grasshopper and BTLx now pin version-critical API facts because training data goes stale exactly where AEC APIs move fastest. The technical estate of a built-environment faculty is becoming software it maintains itself, which moves support, licensing and security responsibility onto the institution. A shared extension repository with named owners, read-before-write ordering and test-on-a-copy discipline is the minimum viable governance for that shift.

descriptionSOURCE: AEC MAGAZINE ↗

Frontier model releases keep arriving faster than institutional evaluation cycles

Google's Gemini 4 Argon, released on 13 October after a September announcement, tops the Vals Index at 68.9 per cent and AutomationBench at 51.3 per cent, above GPT-6 Astra, Claude Fable 5.1 and Claude Opus 5.5 on the published comparison, with introductory pricing of $2 per million input tokens until 30 November. Anthropic positions Opus 5.5 on price, claiming Fable 5.1 parity at 40 per cent less to run than Opus 5. OpenAI shipped its Decisions API with GPT-6 Luna into general availability on 22 October. The capability gap between top models has narrowed to a few points whilst price competition has intensified, which means model choice is now a cost and governance decision more than a quality one. Institutions should hold model-agnostic evaluation harnesses so a cheaper release can be adopted within a term.

Action Required

What this means for your institution:

  • arrow_rightWrite a model credit clause before you accept one. Government has normalised donated and discounted frontier capacity, so any institutional arrangement of that kind needs a named controller, retention and training terms, an evaluation evidence obligation and a defined exit, held in one contract.
  • arrow_rightPut an artefact register under every agent with delegated permissions. The 24-hour notification clock in the Cyber Security and Resilience Bill describes the duty arriving. Log the action, the model version, the authority granted and the outcome in a store your team controls and can query without vendor help.
  • arrow_rightPublish assessment categories at module level. QAA has identified tutor-level variance as the mechanism that puts standards at risk, and the OfS will attach enrolment consequences to teaching quality. Category-per-assessment in curriculum data, visible before enrolment, is the cheapest available risk reduction.
  • arrow_rightBuild forward deployed engineering capacity internally. The pattern the laboratories now sell is an engineer embedded in the customer's operation. Two or three engineers with registry, admissions and studio systems knowledge deliver more verified automation than equivalent external consulting, and the capability stays.
